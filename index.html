Better or worse?

Since about 1980, has life in the U.S. gotten better or worse? For each indicator, make your guess. Then see what the data show.

“About the same” means a change of less than 5% in either direction. A few indicators start later than 1980 because that’s where the data begin; each card says when.

What to make of it

  • Progress isn’t one thing. Some indicators improved dramatically, some got worse, and some barely moved. “Are things getting better?” depends on which ones you look at.
  • Averages hide groups. A national average can improve while some groups fall behind. Ask who gained, and who didn’t.
  • The starting year shapes the story. Homicide fell a lot from 1980 but is about where it was in 2000. Pick a different start and you can tell a different story with the same data.
  • Better isn’t the same as good. An indicator can improve and still be a serious problem.
  • Measurement changes. Surveys and definitions change over time. Each card’s details note the breaks that matter.

Talk about it

  1. Were more of your misses too pessimistic or too optimistic? Why might that be?
  2. Where do your impressions of these trends come from: news, social media, family, classes? How might each source tilt your guesses?
  3. Pick one indicator that improved. What changed: laws, technology, money, culture, or something else?
  4. Pick one that got worse. Who is most affected, and what would it take to reverse it?
  5. Which indicator here would you most want broken down by race, income, age or region? What do you predict you’d see?

A measurement puzzle

Published U.S. maternal mortality rates roughly doubled between 2000 and 2024. But most of that rise comes from how deaths are recorded. Starting in 2003, states gradually added a pregnancy checkbox to death certificates, which caught deaths that were missed before. When CDC researchers compared 2002 and 2018 using the old method, the rates were about the same (8.9 and 8.7 per 100,000).

That’s why maternal mortality isn’t one of the cards. It’s also a good reminder to ask “did the thing change, or did the way we count it change?”
